# [Multi-Base DOA] Multi-base motion target positioning by unknown position and offset mobile transmitters [with MATLAB code]

This paper investigates the problem of multi-baseline position and velocity estimation for moving targets using an unknown cooperative mobile emitter. The measurements are time delays and frequencies, with each measurement having an unknown offset. It is shown that despite the need to estimate parameters such as the emitter's position, velocity, and offset, combining direct path measurements between the emitter and receiver with indirect path measurements from the emitter through the target to the receiver can improve positioning performance. For independent identically distributed Gaussian noise, we derive the geometric conditions necessary to eliminate performance degradation caused by offsets. An algebraic solution for positioning moving targets is developed, and its performance in a Gaussian noise small error region is analyzed to achieve the Cramér-Rao Lower Bound (CRLB) accuracy. Special cases involving only time delay measurements are studied, and an optimal geometric structure for handling unknown emitter positions and time offsets is designed. Simulations validate the theoretical derivations.
Introduction: Multi-baseline positioning methods are favored in radar and sonar applications due to their flexibility, superior performance, and broader coverage compared to single-baseline methods. In recent years, the concept of multi-baseline positioning has been applied in wireless communication and other commercial fields. Multi-baseline positioning utilizes signals transmitted by the emitter to illuminate the target, with a set of receivers separated from the emitter observing the echoes to locate the target. Traditional multi-baseline positioning assumes known emitter and receiver positions, and various solutions have been proposed, including maximum likelihood estimation, closed-form solutions, semidefinite relaxation solutions, etc.
In many applications, the emitter may be non-cooperative, not synchronized with the receiver, and its carrier frequency unknown, leading to unknown time and frequency offsets in the received signal. Additionally, Doppler shifts produced by the motion of the emitter and target can be utilized for better target localization, but introduce new complexities such as unknown emitter speed and frequency measurement offsets. This paper presents a fundamental study on the positioning problem of moving emitter and moving targets with unknown offsets, adding variables such as target and emitter speeds and unknown offsets compared to existing work, and utilizing frequency measurements to propose new algorithms and optimize geometric structures.

Conclusion This study investigates the problem of positioning moving targets using multi-base station time delay and frequency measurement under unknown offsets caused by non-cooperative mobile transmitters. The CRLB analysis shows that combining indirect and direct path measurements to jointly estimate the target, transmitter positions, and time and frequency offsets outperforms the TDOA/FDOA method using only indirect path measurements. Conditions for eliminating performance losses due to unknown offsets are derived, and an algebraic closed-form solution is proposed to achieve CRLB accuracy. Special cases involving only time measurements are studied, and the optimal geometrical positioning is derived. Under the optimal geometry, when the number of sensors is sufficiently large, the degradation in performance due to unknown time offsets can be ignored. Future work will extend the algorithm to scenarios with multiple unknown transmitters and situations where sensor locations have errors, and address the data association issue when multiple transmitters have similar waveforms.
